Why strength, why now

After 50, muscle and strength fade faster every year. Lifting is how you keep them.

Keep your muscle

Lifting weights two to three times a week is one of the best-studied ways to hold on to muscle and strength as you get older.

Look after your joints

Strong muscles support knees, hips, shoulders and back. Jarl swaps in gentler versions when something is sore.

Stay steady

Stronger legs and better balance help you climb stairs, carry the shopping and stay active for years to come.

Adapts to you

Your training changes with how you feel

Before each training, a quick check-in asks how you are. Today’s session adjusts to match.

  • A light day after a bad night, an extra set when you feel strong
  • A calmer session when you’re stressed, the 20-minute version when time is short
  • Joint-friendly versions for sore knees, shoulders or lower back
  • Tell the coach how you feel in your own words, and it suggests a session

A clear plan

Every exercise, shown step by step

Full-body trainings of 20 to 60 minutes, at the gym or at home with dumbbells. Never lifted before, or coming back after years? You start where you are.

  • Moving demos and short, plain cues for every exercise
  • Weights that go up as you get stronger, with a lighter week every six weeks
  • Two to six days a week, on the days that suit you, with rest timers between sets
  • An 8-minute mobility routine and balance work for rest days

Stays fresh

New exercises every six weeks

After each lighter week, a new block begins. Every movement keeps its job but changes form, so training stays balanced and never gets stale.

  • Goblet squat becomes split squat, then step-up. Your weights carry over
  • Try something different: pick another exercise for the same muscles
  • Don’t like one? Hide it, and Jarl replaces it everywhere

Questions

Who is Jarl for?

Men over 50 who want to get or stay strong, whether you’re new to weights or coming back after a break.

Do I need a gym?

No. Choose gym or home when you set up. At home, a set of dumbbells is enough, and Jarl picks exercises that work with them.

I have bad knees, shoulders or back. Can I still train?

Jarl has a joint-friendly version of most exercises. Set it once in your settings, or tap what’s sore in the check-in before each training. Sharp or lasting pain is a reason to see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Do I need an Apple Watch?

No. Jarl works on its own. With an Apple Watch, a Garmin that broadcasts heart rate or a chest strap, you see your pulse live during training.

Which devices does it work on?

Any iPhone or iPad with iOS 15 or later. The AI coach needs an iPhone with Apple Intelligence. Without it, Jarl uses its own built-in rules.

Is this medical advice?

No. Jarl gives general training guidance and does not diagnose or treat any condition. Talk to your doctor before you start a new exercise programme, especially if you have heart problems, high blood pressure or recent surgery.
